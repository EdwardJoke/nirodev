### 2. LG smart TVs caught logging audio with screens off
LG smart TVs were found to be logging audio even when the screen was off and actively snooping on local network devices, researchers reported via Notebookcheck. A Hacker News discussion highlighted a video estimating the issue affects 216 million televisions worldwide. The behavior raises serious questions about always-on microphone access and data transmission in household entertainment devices. [Read more](https://www.notebookcheck.net/LG-smart-TVs-caught-logging-audio-with-screen-off-and-snooping-on-local-devices.1391214.0.html)

:::brief

LG smart TVs, including OLED G5 models, were found scanning local networks and recording microphone audio even when the screen appeared off, with data sent to LG's ad division.

- Researchers from Gamers Nexus and Level1Techs found the TVs gather device names, Wi-Fi signal strengths, and location data.
- LG Ad Solutions claims access to 363 million secondary addressable devices in the US alone.
- Remote code execution vulnerabilities in webOS were also discovered; researchers recommend disconnecting LG TVs from the internet.

:::


### 3. Switzerland is replacing Microsoft on 3,000 computers
Switzerland's federal government launched a pilot program to replace Microsoft software on approximately 3,000 computers, according to IT's FOSS, widely discussed on Hacker News. The move signals growing interest among government bodies in reducing dependency on a single proprietary stack. The pilot will likely evaluate open-source or domestically developed alternatives for productivity and infrastructure workloads. [Read more](https://itsfoss.com/news/switzerland-replace-microssoft-pilot/)

:::brief

Switzerland's federal government has launched a pilot program to replace Microsoft 365 with the open-source platform openDesk across 3,000 workstations, aiming for full migration by end of 2027.

- The pilot follows a successful proof-of-concept with 172 employees; the military is migrating separately by October 2026.
- CHF 9 million is being invested in the rollout.
- The move is driven by concerns over US cloud legislation, vendor dependency, and rising licensing costs under the 2024 EMBAG Law.

### 9. EU finds smartphone makers ignoring repairability rules
Smartphone manufacturers are largely failing to comply with the EU's repairability requirements, according to The Register's coverage of the issue on Hacker News. The findings underscore a gap between regulatory intent and industry practice, as major vendors continue to design devices that resist user repair. Stricter enforcement mechanisms may be needed to close the gap before the next compliance window closes. [Read more](https://www.theregister.com/personal-tech/2026/09/07/smartphone-makers-dont-bother-to-comply-with-eu-repairability-requirements/5294532)

:::brief

More than 80% of smartphones sold in the EU still lack required repair information, a year after the bloc's repairability rules took effect in June 2025.

- Only about 18% of 2,334 reviewed smartphone records list a URL for spare parts or repair instructions.
- Some manufacturers give themselves class A repairability scores despite being non-compliant.
- Spare parts prices are sometimes listed with absurdly wide ranges, such as €14 to €128 for a battery.

:::
