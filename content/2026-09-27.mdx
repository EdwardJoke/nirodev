The incident, discussed on Hacker News with 295 points and 255 comments, was framed as a failure of basic duty of care rather than a clever hack. For a community built on trust in text editors, the episode raised uncomfortable questions about how much risk users absorb by default. [Read more](https://unsung.aresluna.org/they-had-no-concept-of-a-duty-of-care-to-their-users/)

:::brief

Computer scientist David Chisnall reports that NeoVim deleted his vim persistent undo files when it changed the file format, without migrating or preserving the old data. When he raised the issue, he was told the format was unstable and users should not rely on data being preserved.

- Chisnall has used vim since around 2000 and written five books, a PhD thesis, and over 150 articles with it.
- NeoVim did not upgrade the old undo files or use a different filename—it simply deleted them.
- Chisnall cited Raskin's First Law: a program may not harm a user's data or allow it to come to harm.

A new arXiv paper from DeepSeek introduces DSec, an elastic compute framework designed to dynamically allocate resources across distributed workloads. The paper attracted 311 points and 100 comments on Hacker News, signaling strong interest in the Chinese lab's continued output. The approach could offer implications for reducing infrastructure costs in large-scale model training. [Read more](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22978)

:::brief

DeepSeek published a paper on DSec, a production sandbox platform for large-scale agentic LLM training and evaluation. A single unit spans around 160 nodes, serving about 3 million sandboxes per day with over 380,000 concurrent sandboxes and 5,000 creations per second.

- DSec exposes FnCall, container, microVM, and full-VM sandbox backends through a unified SDK.
- The platform decouples stateful rollout execution from preemptible GPU training to preserve rollout state.
- It loads image data on demand from Fire-Flyer File System (3FS), a cluster-wide distributed filesystem.

### 10. AI Chat Templates Change How Models Refer to Themselves
Researchers published findings showing that switching a language model's chat template can alter its self-referential voice, effectively changing how the model describes itself in conversation. The paper, posted on arXiv and discussed on Hacker News with 96 points and 99 comments, adds to a growing body of work on how interface-level choices shape model behavior in subtle but measurable ways. [Read more](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25021)

:::brief

A new arXiv paper finds that chat templates act as a switch controlling how LLMs refer to themselves: when present, templates amplify disclaimer language like 'I'm just an AI' and suppress experiential voice. Researchers can steer this behavior by manipulating a direction in the model's activation space.

- The study tested eight popular open-source instruct models up to 9B parameters.
- Adding the disclaimer direction to models without a chat template made them disclaim like the template was present.
- The authors caution that model self-descriptions should not be treated literally because they are partly set by the chat template.

:::
