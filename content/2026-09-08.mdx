### 1. Mistral closes €3b sovereign-ai raise at €21b valuation

Samsung, Scaleup Europe, and PSG Equity led a Series D round that values the French lab at €21 billion, according to TechCrunch. The company is positioning its open-weight models as an alternative to closed systems, arguing that enterprises and governments prefer models they can audit and run on domestic infrastructure. The capital will fund both frontier research and the commercial distribution of those weights. [Read more](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/08/mistral-raises-e3b-as-sovereign-ai-becomes-big-business/)

:::brief

Mistral AI raised €3 billion in a Series D at a post-money valuation exceeding €21 billion, the largest equity round ever by a European technology company. Samsung Electronics led the round with EQT and PSG Equity as co-leads. The company operates in 20 countries.

- Series D, €3B raised, €21B+ post-money valuation
- Led by Samsung; EQT and PSG Equity co-leads
- Largest equity fundraising by a European tech company

### 2. Google secures $1.9b loan to revive a closed Iowa nuclear plant

Google's Homer City plant received a $1.9 billion loan from the U.S. Department of Energy, TechCrunch reports. The facility had been shut down before Google moved to restart it, and the federal backing marks one of the largest government commitments to nuclear capacity tied to a single private operator. The plant is intended to supply electricity for Google's AI workloads. [Read more](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/08/googles-revived-nuclear-power-plant-gets-1-9b-loan-from-us-government/)

:::brief

The U.S. Department of Energy approved a $1.9 billion loan to NextEra Energy to refurbish Google's Duane Arnold Energy Center nuclear plant in Iowa, with a 2029 restart target. It is the second such loan, following a $1 billion loan to Constellation Energy for the Three Mile Island reactor.

- $1.9B DOE loan to NextEra Energy for Iowa plant
- Restart targeted for 2029; 50MW set aside for local cooperative
- Second DOE loan after $1B to Constellation for Three Mile Island

:::


### 3. Chrome ships security patches every two weeks instead of monthly

Google is compressing Chrome's release cadence from one month to two weeks, citing an AI-intensified security landscape. The change means both vulnerability fixes and feature rollouts will move faster, reducing the window in which exposed browsers sit unpatched. [Read more](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/08/chrome-is-now-shipping-updates-every-2-weeks-as-ai-changes-the-security-landscape/)

:::brief

Google has switched Chrome to a two-week release schedule with Chrome 153, down from four weeks, citing the need for faster security patching in the AI era. Mozilla, Microsoft, and Brave have followed with similar two-week schedules.

- Chrome 153 launched on desktop, iOS, and Android
- Reduced from 4-week to 2-week cycle; was 6 weeks until 2021
- Mozilla, Microsoft, and Brave adopted 2-week schedules
