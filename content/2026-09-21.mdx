### 1. Google hit with $463 million EU fine over location data

Hardly the first time it's run afoul of EU regulations, Google has been fined $463 million for breaching European location data rules, according to Engadget. The penalty underscores the bloc's renewed enforcement appetite against tech giants who collect user positioning data without adequate consent. [Read more](https://www.engadget.com/2263850/google-fined-dollar463-million-for-breaching-eu-location-data-rules/)

:::brief

Ireland's Data Protection Commission fined Google €403 million ($463 million) for improperly processing location data under GDPR rules across Web & App Activity, Location History, and Location Accuracy features. Google must comply within six months and stated the policies have since been updated.

- The DPC found violations between May 2018 and February 2020; the fine is the fourth-largest GDPR penalty it has issued.
- The largest was a $1.3 billion fine against Meta for US data transfers.
- Google faces three other ongoing large-scale statutory inquiries.

:::


### 2. Apple's Siri AI settlement opens for iPhone owners

Apple is paying $250 million to settle claims that it failed to deliver on AI-upgraded Siri features, and eligible iPhone owners can now submit a claim. If you purchased an iPhone 15 Pro, iPhone 15 Pro Max, or any iPhone 16 model between June 10th, 2024, and are in the US, you may qualify for a payout. The settlement acknowledges consumers felt misled by promises of enhanced AI capabilities. [Read more](https://www.theverge.com/tech/998191/apple-siri-ai-iphone-16-class-action-lawsuit-settlement)

:::brief

Apple is paying $250 million to settle a class-action lawsuit claiming it failed to deliver AI-upgraded Siri on iPhone 16 devices marketed as built for Apple Intelligence. Eligible US customers who bought an iPhone 15 Pro, iPhone 15 Pro Max, or iPhone 16 model between June 2024 and March 2025 can file claims by December 21, 2026.

- Payouts are estimated at $25 per device, potentially rising to $95 depending on claim volume.
- Applicants must provide their name, contact info, and iPhone serial number.
- The delayed Siri feature arrived with iOS 27 this month; Apple denies wrongdoing.

### 5. Nintendo's Game & Watch born from a calculator and boredom

The Game & Watch line was one of Nintendo's most successful early game projects, and the man behind it was inspired from an unlikely source—a calculator and sheer boredom. Engadget traces how this accidental innovation became a cornerstone of handheld gaming history, showing how constraints can spark creativity. [Read more](https://www.engadget.com/2260782/nintendo-game-watch-explained/)

:::brief

Nintendo's Game & Watch was conceived by Gunpei Yokoi after he saw a salaryman playing with a calculator on a train in the late 1970s. The handheld used LCD technology similar to Sharp calculators, and a timely pitch to president Hiroshi Yamauchi led to a collaboration with Sharp.

- Yokoi joined Nintendo in 1965 when the company was struggling; the Ultra Hand toy sold 1.4 million units.
- The first Game & Watch, Ball, released in 1980 using a chip capable of displaying 72 segments.
- The innovation marked Nintendo's pivot into the digital handheld gaming market.

:::
