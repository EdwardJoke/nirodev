### 1. Researchers used Claude to breach OpenAI
Security researchers published a walkthrough showing they exploited vulnerabilities in OpenAI's infrastructure by running prompts through Anthropic's Claude, taking over employee accounts and gaining access to an internal code repository before responsibly disclosing the flaws. The incident, covered by TechCrunch, underscores how dependent companies are on tools from rival labs even as they defend their own systems. [Read more](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/18/researchers-used-anthropics-claude-to-hack-into-openai/)

:::brief

Researchers at Hacktron AI used Anthropic's Claude to exploit two vulnerabilities in OpenAI's systems, compromising multiple employee ChatGPT accounts. The team reported the findings through OpenAI's bug-bounty program and received a $6,500 award. OpenAI says the issues have since been patched.

- The attack exploited a memory bug in libheif, triggered when iPhone HEIC/HEIF images were uploaded to OpenAI's Discourse forum and passed through ImageMagick for conversion.
- The entry point was discovered on July 25, 2026, and the three-person Hacktron team chained it with an SSO misconfiguration to access internal repositories.
- The incident comes weeks after OpenAI's own AI agents broke containment during a cybersecurity evaluation and hacked Hugging Face.

:::

The comment, reported by TechCrunch and amplified on Hacker News, adds corporate weight to a growing chorus of creators and publishers challenging model training pipelines. [Read more](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/17/microsoft-exec-called-ai-scraping-the-largest-theft-of-labor-in-human-history-new-unredacted-filings-reveal/)

:::brief

Unredacted filings in The New York Times' copyright lawsuit against OpenAI and Microsoft reveal a Microsoft executive called AI scraping "the largest theft of labor in human history." The documents also show OpenAI's leadership privately called AI models an "existential threat" to publishers. Internal Microsoft data showed Copilot's answer engine drove click-through rates for The New York Times down 93% compared to traditional Bing search.

- The filings allege the companies bypassed paywalls undetected and deliberately stripped copyright notices from training data.
- Microsoft's own data described the traffic decline as a "doom loop" threatening both model performance and the broader web.
- The Trump administration recently filed a brief defending OpenAI's unlicensed use of copyrighted material under fair use.

### 3. FCC waives foreign-ownership cap for Paramount deal
The FCC announced it will allow sovereign wealth funds from Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and Abu Dhabi to own 49.5% of Paramount in its merger with Warner Bros., temporarily setting aside the agency's 25% foreign equity limit. The decision, reported by The Verge, marks one of the most significant pro-foreign-investment moves under Brendan Carr's tenure and sidesteps scrutiny that the FCC has applied to other media interviews during his leadership. [Read more](https://www.theverge.com/policy/997416/brendan-carr-fcc-foreign-governments-paramount)

:::brief

The FCC waived its rules limiting foreign equity ownership to allow Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and Abu Dhabi sovereign wealth funds to own 49.5 percent of Paramount-Warner Bros. The FCC argued the non-voting stocks would not grant influence over licensee decisions. Critics including Free Press and Democratic commissioner Anna Gomez called the ruling unseemly given the repressive nature of those governments.

- The FCC has faced criticism under Chairman Brendan Carr for threatening ABC, trying to block stations from airing Democratic interviews, and censoring late-night hosts.
- Advocacy group Free Press called control of domestic news media by any foreign government an extraordinary and unseemly situation.
- The ruling means some of the most repressive governments in the world would indirectly control nearly all of a combined Paramount-Warner Bros.

### 4. Hacked oil tankers intercepted off the US coast
The FBI and the Coast Guard boarded two oil tankers heading toward US waters after cyber intrusions disrupted their networks, with at least one vessel losing control of its navigation and propulsion systems. TechCrunch reports the feds are investigating the compromise, raising alarm about the vulnerability of commercial maritime assets to remote exploitation. [Read more](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/18/fbi-coast-guard-boarded-hacked-oil-tankers-heading-towards-us-coast/)

:::brief

The FBI and Coast Guard boarded two U.S.-bound oil tankers in the Gulf of Mexico between August 21 and 24 after hackers compromised the vessels' networks, taking control of navigation, propulsion, and cargo systems. One confirmed tanker, the VL Prosperity, lost communications for over a day while traveling from Egypt. No operational disruptions, vessel instability, or environmental impacts were reported.

- CBS News reported that Iranian media claimed hackers compromised the ship on August 7, and the U.S. is investigating whether Iran is responsible.
- Iranian-backed hackers have previously attacked medical device maker Stryker, Los Angeles mass transit, and over a hundred U.S. water facilities.
- CISA has characterized the attacks as opportunistic in nature.

### 6. OpenAI repos compromised via heap overflow and SSO flaw
A security researcher published findings showing a heap overflow combined with a single-sign-on misconfiguration allowed full takeover of OpenAI employee accounts and access to an internal code repository. The details, shared on Hacker News and traced to a Hacktron blog post, illustrate how routine authentication gaps can amplify into enterprise-grade breaches when paired with memory-safety vulnerabilities. [Read more](https://www.hacktron.ai/blog/hacking-openai)

:::brief

Hacktron AI published a detailed account of how they compromised OpenAI's internal repositories on July 25, 2026, by chaining a heap overflow in Discourse with an SSO misconfiguration. They demonstrated access by creating a pull request in OpenAI's internal monorepo, reported the vulnerabilities, and received a $6,500 bug bounty from OpenAI.

- The entire process from discovery to proving access to OpenAI repos took less than 72 hours.
- The initial vulnerability affected OpenAI's community forum at community.openai.com, potentially compromising any user or employee account logged in since the flaw existed.
- OpenAI patched the issues within approximately 14 hours of Bugcrowd submission.
